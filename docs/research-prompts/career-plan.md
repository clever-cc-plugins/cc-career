# Research Prompt: career-plan

**For:** `cc-career:career-plan`
**Current knowledge base:** `plugins/cc-career/skills/_shared/career-planning-frameworks.md` —
Edgar Schein's Career Anchors and the Skill-Will Matrix, applied from general
knowledge with no dedicated research doc behind them yet.
**Why research helps:** validate these two frameworks against current
career-development research, surface any well-evidenced complementary
frameworks, and ground the skill's milestone/timeline guidance in something
more concrete than general knowledge.

Paste the prompt below into a deep-research AI tool (Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini,
Perplexity, or similar). Save the response as Markdown, e.g. to
`docs/research/career-plan.md`, then use it to refine
`career-planning-frameworks.md`.

---

```
─────────────────────────────────────────────
Research prompt: career planning and development frameworks
─────────────────────────────────────────────
You are a research analyst investigating evidence-based frameworks for
individual career planning and skill development, for use in a coaching/
career-planning tool.

Cover, with headers separating each area:

1. Edgar Schein's Career Anchors model — current academic standing, common
   critiques, and how practitioners today identify a person's anchor from a
   short conversation rather than the full formal inventory.
2. The Skill-Will Matrix (and related will/skill or competence/commitment
   matrices used in coaching and management) — origin, evidence base, and
   how it's applied to sequence near-term development priorities.
3. Goal-setting theory relevant to individual career milestones (e.g. Locke &
   Latham's goal-setting theory) — what makes a career milestone likely to be
   achieved vs. abandoned, and how specificity/difficulty/timeframe affect
   follow-through.
4. Any well-evidenced alternative or complementary frameworks for career
   direction-setting (e.g. Ikigai, job crafting, career capital theory) worth
   considering alongside Career Anchors — with an honest assessment of which
   have real evidence behind them vs. which are popular but weakly supported.
5. Common failure modes in self-directed career planning (why plans stall,
   get abandoned, or produce the wrong milestones) and what research says
   reduces them.

Require credible, verifiable sources throughout, with in-text citations —
prioritize peer-reviewed research and well-established practitioner sources
over blog-level content. Provide a formatted source list in APA style at the
end. Respond in Markdown.
─────────────────────────────────────────────
```
