# Personal Branding Frameworks — Shared Reference

Shared reference for `personal-branding-strategy`. Apply a framework whenever the
task involves articulating how a person wants to be perceived professionally, or the
themes their public output (posts, talks, writing) should reinforce. For a narrowly
scoped ask — e.g. rewriting a single bio line — skip this file and answer directly.

## How to select

1. **Identify the branding challenge** from the brief (e.g. clarify a scattered
   online presence, decide what to be known for before a job search, prepare talking
   points ahead of speaking engagements).
2. **Note the available context** — career background and goals, and career-plan.md
   if registered (prefer its Career Anchor finding as an input, not a re-derivation).
3. **Check for project preferences** — a recorded rejected framing in
   `.claude/learnings.md` wins over this file's default.
4. **Find the matching framework** using the descriptions below. Both are usually
   applied together — Golden Circle sets the message, Personal SWOT stress-tests it
   against reality — state each choice separately when both apply.
5. **State the choice** before generating: `"Using [framework] because [one-sentence
reason]."`

---

## Golden Circle (Simon Sinek), applied personally

**Ground the brand message in Why before How or What**

What it is: Sinek's model orders communication from the inside out — Why (the
belief or purpose driving the work), How (the distinctive approach), What (the
visible output: job title, projects, content). Applied to a person rather than a
company, it prevents a personal brand from collapsing into a job-title list.

Best for: The first branding pass for someone whose current online presence is just
a resume restated; clarifying the throughline across a varied career; giving
cc-content skills a "Why" to keep every piece of content on-message.

Avoid: A person who already has a clear, working Why and just needs tactical themes
— skip straight to naming the themes.

How to apply: (1) From the loaded career background and career-plan (if present,
its Career Anchor), draft one Why sentence — the belief, not the job. (2) Draft one
How sentence — the distinctive method or lens. (3) List the current and near-term
What — roles, projects, content — and check each still traces back to the Why; flag
any that don't.

## Personal SWOT

**Stress-test the brand message against internal and external reality**

What it is: A four-quadrant audit — Strengths and Weaknesses (internal, about the
person), Opportunities and Threats (external, about the market or industry they
operate in). Applied after a Why/How/What draft, it catches an aspirational brand
message that isn't yet backed by evidence, or that ignores a real market shift.

Best for: Pressure-testing a draft brand message before publishing; identifying
which Strength to lead with when several are true; flagging a Threat (e.g. an
oversaturated niche) the person should position around rather than into.

Avoid: A first pass with no draft message yet to test — run Golden Circle first.

How to apply: (1) List 2-4 Strengths from the loaded career background that are
demonstrable, not aspirational. (2) List 1-2 Weaknesses honestly — gaps the brand
message should not overclaim past. (3) List 1-2 Opportunities in the person's
market (a niche going underserved, a format gaining reach). (4) List 1-2 Threats
(a crowded niche, a skill becoming commoditized). (5) Adjust the draft brand themes
so they lead with a Strength that meets an Opportunity, and avoid overclaiming past
a named Weakness.
